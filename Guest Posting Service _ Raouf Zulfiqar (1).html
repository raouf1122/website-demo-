Guest posting in plain words

A guest post is an article you publish on someone else's website. The site gets useful content for its readers. You get exposure to a new audience and a link back to your own website. When the site and your business are in the same field, the link tells search engines and readers that your page is relevant to that topic.

The hard part is not writing. It is choosing the right site, writing something that actually helps its readers, and linking in a way that looks natural. Many people lose money by chasing the biggest number on a metric tool and ignoring relevance. My job is to help you avoid that.

Guest post or link insertion?

A guest post is a brand new page. You can shape the topic and the text around your link, and the article can bring readers on its own. It takes longer because the article has to be written, reviewed and scheduled by the site.

Link insertion adds your link to a page that already exists. It is usually faster, and the page may already have visitors. The trade-off is control: the text around your link was written for another purpose, so you must pick pages whose topic really fits.

Many clients start with a few guest posts and add link insertions later. There is no single right answer, and I can suggest a mix once I know your niche and goal.

Guest Posting Guide

How guest posting works, the mistakes that waste money, and how to get more from every link.

What guest posting is

Guest posting means writing an article for another website and getting a link back to yours. Done well, you reach that site's readers, build trust in your brand and earn a relevant link. Done badly, you waste money and can attract unwanted attention.

How it works, step by step

  1. Set the goal

    Decide which page you want to support (home, service, blog post) and what keyword or brand you care about.

  2. Pick the niche

    Choose sites whose topic matches your page. A tech tool linked from a tech blog is natural. The same link from a cooking blog is not.

  3. Check the site

    Open it. Is it indexed (search site:domain.com)? Is there recent, real content? Does traffic look genuine in a tool like Ahrefs or Similarweb? Are there too many unrelated outbound links?

  4. Prepare the article

    Original, useful and written for the site's readers. Usually 800 to 1,500 words. One contextual link to your page is enough, plus one trusted outside source if it helps the reader.

  5. Plan the anchor text

    Decide the clickable words before writing. Mix branded, URL and natural phrases. See the table below.

  6. Publish

    The site's editor reviews and publishes. I send you the live URL as soon as it is up.

  7. Verify and track

    Open the URL. Confirm the link goes to the right page with the right anchor, the page is not noindex, and note the link attribute. Log it in a sheet and re-check monthly.

Common mistakes

Choosing by DA/DR only

DA and DR are third-party scores, not Google's. A high score on an unrelated or traffic-less site helps little.

Irrelevant niches

Links from sites unrelated to your topic look unnatural and bring the wrong readers.

Exact-match anchors everywhere

Using the same money keyword each time is a clear pattern. Vary your anchors.

Thin or duplicate articles

Spun, copied or very short posts rarely get indexed and can hurt the site and you.

Too many links in one post

Stuffing several commercial links makes the article read like an ad. One good link is better.

Content-farm sites

Sites that accept every topic, post daily and show no traffic are a poor investment.

Buying a huge batch at once

Fifty links in a week is a spike. Spread links over weeks and months.

Never checking afterwards

Links can be removed, changed to nofollow or the page deindexed. Verify and keep a record.

How to get better results

  • Put relevance first, metrics second.
  • Link to useful pages, not only your homepage.
  • Write for readers. Helpful articles get indexed and clicked.
  • Mix site types and publish gradually.
  • Be patient. Results usually take weeks, not days.
  • Support links with solid on-site SEO: clear pages, fast loading, good content.

Link types

A dofollow link can pass ranking signals. Nofollow, sponsored and ugc tell search engines to treat the link differently. A natural profile has a mix of both.

What the metrics mean

DA and DR are estimates from Moz and Ahrefs. Use them to filter, then check real traffic, indexing and content quality yourself.

Google's guidelines say links bought to manipulate rankings can be treated as spam. Choose relevant sites, use natural anchors and publish real content that helps readers.

Why guest posting works

Search engines look at links as one signal that other sites find a page useful. A link from a relevant, trusted site in your field carries more meaning than one from an unrelated place. A guest post also puts your name in front of real readers who may visit your site, remember your brand or search for it later. That combination of a relevant link and new readers is the real value, not a number on a metric tool.

What a good guest post looks like

  • A headline that fits the site and promises something useful.
  • An opening that gets to the point quickly.
  • Clear headings so the article is easy to scan on a phone.
  • Real examples, steps or data, not filler.
  • One natural link where it truly helps the reader.
  • Careful proofreading, correct facts and original wording.

Writing tips that help you get published

Read five recent posts on the site first. Match the tone, length and formatting. Pick a topic the site has not covered, and make it more useful than what is already there. Avoid sales language. A reader should learn something even if they never click your link.

A simple 90 day plan

  1. Weeks 1 to 2: audit and plan

    Choose the pages to support, list your niche topics and decide anchor text. Check your own site is fast and has solid content.

  2. Weeks 3 to 6: first placements

    Place two to four links on relevant sites. Verify each live URL and log it.

  3. Weeks 7 to 10: review

    Check whether the pages are indexed, look at referral visitors and compare rankings. Adjust anchors and sites.

  4. Weeks 11 to 13: scale carefully

    Add more placements gradually, reuse the sites that worked and refresh older content.

How to measure results

Link building works slowly, so track the right things and give it time.

  • Search Console: watch impressions, clicks and the queries that bring people to your pages. The Links report shows sites that link to you.
  • Analytics: look at referral traffic from the sites where you published.
  • Rankings: track a small set of target keywords weekly in a sheet.
  • Brand searches: more people searching your name is a good sign.
  • Link log: record the URL, anchor, date and link type, and re-check monthly.

Expect weeks, not days. Results also depend on your own content, your competition and how healthy your site is.

Red flags when choosing a site

Every topic accepted

A site that publishes on any subject has no real audience.

No recent posts

Dead sites rarely get crawled and pass little value.

Link-heavy articles

If each post sends readers to a different company, your link is just one more ad.

Traffic collapse

A sharp drop can mean a penalty or lost trust.

Hidden "sponsored" everywhere

Heavy commercial content is a sign of a link farm.

Pressure to buy fast

A good site does not need you to rush.

FAQ

Quick answers to the questions I hear most.

What is guest posting?

You publish an article on another website in your niche. The article is useful to that site's readers and includes a link back to your website.

Can I choose the website?

Yes. I share matching sites with their rates and you pick. You can also send me a site you already have in mind and I will check if it is available.

Do I need to send my own article?

You can send one, or order writing from me. Either way the article should be original and relevant to the site.

How long does publishing take?

It depends on the site's editor. I tell you the expected time before you confirm the order.

Will the link be dofollow?

It depends on the site, and some sites mark sponsored links as nofollow. I tell you the link type before you order so you know what to expect.

Can you guarantee rankings?

No one honestly can. Guest posts can support your SEO when they are relevant and natural, but rankings also depend on your content, competition and the rest of your site.

How many links should I buy?

Start small. A few relevant links, spread over weeks, are safer and easier to measure than a large batch.

How do I pay?

After you confirm the site and the rate on WhatsApp, I share the payment details.

What if my link is removed?

Send me the URL and I will check with the site. Re-checking links monthly helps you catch this early.

How do I know a website is real?

Open it and look around. Check the date of the latest post, whether the topics are consistent and whether the articles read like they were written for people. I also check these before I suggest a site.

What is link insertion?

It means adding your link to an existing article on a site. It can be faster than a new guest post, but the text around the link was not written for you, so the topic must fit.

Can I link to more than one page?

Yes, but keep it natural. Usually one contextual link to the most relevant page is enough. Spread different links across different posts.

How long until I see results?

Often several weeks. The post must be published, found and indexed, and search engines need time to reassess your pages. Nothing is instant.

Do you accept every niche?

Each site sets its own rules, and some do not accept certain topics. I tell you up front if a site will not take your niche, so you do not waste time.

Should I use the same anchor text every time?

No. Repeating the same keyword looks unnatural. Mix branded, URL, natural and generic anchors. The guide has a table you can follow.
